# Israeli University Admission Guide

## Overview

Israeli university admissions are primarily based on a combined score called the **Sekhem** (ציון סכם), which merges the Bagrut matriculation average with the Psychometric Entrance Test (PET) score. Each university and program has its own weighting formula and minimum thresholds.

## There is no national weighting formula

Earlier versions of this file listed "~40% Bagrut, ~60% Psychometric" against every
university. That figure is not published by any of them and should not be quoted.
What the institutions actually publish, verified 2026-08-27:

- **Tel Aviv University** states that the weighting formula between the Bagrut average
  and the psychometric score changes from year to year, and publishes a two-stage
  linear transformation of the adapted Bagrut average and the psychometric score
  rather than a percentage split.
- **The Technion** publishes admission formulas **per faculty**, not one
  institution-wide formula.
- **The Hebrew University** publishes the score components and the bonus conditions,
  but no weighting numbers.

Send the applicant to the target institution's own sekhem calculator. Any figure this
skill produces is an orientation estimate, not the number the university will compute.

## Verified 5-unit bonus tables

These are the institutions' own published tables, read 2026-08-27. Both award the
bonus only at a subject grade of **60 or above**. Do not carry either table across
to a third institution.

**Technion** (bonus at grade 60+, 5 units):

| Subject group (5 units) | Standard | Under the science/technology combination |
|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | +30 | +30 |
| Physics, Chemistry | +25 | +30 |
| Biology | +25 | +25 |
| Recognized technological subjects | +25 | +20 |
| Literature, Bible, History, English, Arabic, Hebrew-for-Arabic-speakers | +25 | not applicable |
| Other listed subjects | +20 | not applicable |

The enlarged combination bonus applies to candidates holding 5-unit Math plus either two 5-unit science subjects or one science and one technological subject at 5 units. The Technion also counts **Mathematics units double** in the best-average calculation (4 units count as 8, 5 units as 10) for every track except architecture and landscape architecture.

**Tel Aviv University** (bonus at grade 60+; a grade given for a combination of subjects earns nothing):

| Subject | Units | Bonus |
|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | 5 | +35 |
| English, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Arabic, Literature, History, Bible | 5 | +25 |
| English and Mathematics | 4 | +12.5 |
| Other subjects on TAU's bonus list | 5 / 4 | +20 / +10 |

**Cross-institution traps.** The +12.5 for 4-unit English is a TAU figure; the
Technion's 4-unit bonus for its "other listed subjects" is +10. Computer Science is
not named in TAU's 5-unit +25 row, so do not assume it qualifies. TAU awards nothing
for a grade given for a combination of subjects (for example Hebrew expression
combined with Hebrew literature).

## The best (optimal) average

Universities do not average every subject on the certificate. They compute a best
average, dropping the subjects that lower it. TAU's own worked example takes a
straight average of 97.81 across 32 units and, after dropping three subjects, reports
an adapted average of **108.39** across 23 units. An estimate built on a plain
all-subject average therefore understates the applicant, sometimes substantially.

## The December 2026 psychometric change, from the universities' side

Tel Aviv University has published its rule; most others had not at the time of
writing. At TAU, applying from the תשפ"ח registration season as a **two-year pilot**:

- Sat only the old three-domain format: TAU takes the **higher** of the three-domain
  weighted score and the recomputed two-domain (quantitative + verbal) score. NITE
  publishes both from October 2026 in the examinee's personal area.
- Sat only the new format: scored on the two-domain result, with no English component.
- Sat both: the highest of all three scores is used.

Do not present this as a sector-wide rule. Each institution decides independently.

## Admission Tracks

### Standard Track (Bagrut + Psychometric)
- Most common path
- Sekhem = weighted combination of Bagrut average and PET score
- Each university calculates the "optimal" combination that maximizes applicant's score

### Psychometric-Only Track
- For students with exceptional PET scores (typically 700+)
- Available at some universities for some programs
- Does not require a full Bagrut certificate
- Usually requires higher PET score than the standard track cutoff

### Mechina (Preparatory Program)
- For students without a full Bagrut or with low scores
- Typically 1-year program
- Successful completion may qualify for university admission
- Available at most universities

### Foreign Credentials Track
- For students with non-Israeli high school diplomas
- Accepts IB, AP, A-Levels, SAT, and other international credentials
- Each university has specific conversion tables

sekhem calculator, and say plainly that cutoffs move year to year with the applicant

## Tips for Maximizing Admission Chances

1. **Re-sitting the PET is possible but not free or unlimited.** NITE sets its own rules on repeat sittings, and whether an institution uses the best of several scores is that institution's policy, not a national rule. Confirm both before advising a re-sit.
2. **Some institutions compute a best average** by dropping the subjects that lower it (TAU documents this and publishes a worked example). Do not assume every institution does, and do not assume any of them try alternative bagrut/psychometric weightings on the applicant's behalf.
3. **5-unit subjects matter**, but only where the target institution's own table pays a bonus for that subject, and only at a grade of 60 or above.
4. **Apply to several programmes**, since cutoffs vary by institution, by faculty and by year.
5. **Consider the routes that are not "university on a sekhem".** A mechina (preparatory year) helps a borderline applicant. Beyond that, most Israeli undergraduates study at colleges (michlalot) rather than the research universities, and the Open University admits to a first course with no bagrut certificate and no psychometric, which is the standard route for an applicant with a weak or missing certificate and can lead to a transfer. This skill does not cover college or Open University admission mechanics; route the applicant to those institutions directly.
6. **Check programme-specific requirements** - some require particular Bagrut subjects (Chemistry for medicine, for instance).
7. **Discharged soldiers**: completing or improving a bagrut after the army runs through the external-examinee route described above. The funding side (the chayal meshuchrar deposit, IDF-funded completion programmes, mechinot for released soldiers) is a separate entitlement system that this skill does not cover and does not attempt to summarise; it is time-limited, so route the applicant to the unit for the guidance of discharged soldiers rather than guessing at eligibility windows.

## Psychometric sessions, fees and the AMIRNET test

### Published test dates

Sessions currently published by NITE (dates are subject to change; re-read the source before advising):

| Test date | Languages offered | Registration closes | Score forecast |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2-3.9.2026 | Hebrew, Arabic | 8.7.2026 | 18.10.2026 |
| 4, 6.12.2026 (first two-domain session) | Hebrew, Arabic | 14.10.2026 | 20.1.2027 |
| 18-19.4.2027 | Hebrew, Arabic, combined Hebrew-English, Russian, French | 10.2.2027 | not yet published |
| 1.7.2027 | Hebrew, Arabic, combined Hebrew-English, Russian, French | 13.5.2027 | not yet published |

Three things to flag:

- **December 2026 is Hebrew and Arabic only**, on two non-consecutive days. A student needing Russian, French or the combined version cannot sit until April 2027.
- **The registration-close date is also the accommodations deadline, and NITE runs its own accommodations track.** A Ministry Bagrut accommodation does NOT carry over: NITE has a separate application ("bechina betnaim mutamim") and its own decision. A student assuming it transfers sits without extra time and cannot repair it, since the deadline passed months earlier and sessions run only a few times a year.
- NITE publishes a per-session score-forecast date, not a fixed turnaround, so do not promise "results in 45 days"; read the forecast for that session (December 2026 is 47 days out).

Sessions can be postponed for security or holiday reasons. Confirm against `https://www.nite.org.il/test-dates-and-prices/`.


### Fee tables

**There are two fee tables, and the reform makes the test cheaper.** Quote the one matching the student's test date:

   | Fee item | Three-domain test (through Sept 2026) | Two-domain test (from Dec 2026) |
   |---|---|---|
   | Standard registration | 665 NIS | **495 NIS** |
   | Late registration* | 1,000 NIS | **745 NIS** |
   | Registration on test day* | 1,665 NIS | **1,240 NIS** |
   | Changing the test date | 120 NIS | **89 NIS** |

   *Late and same-day registration are not offered at every session. The date-change fee is 18% of the exam fee, which is where both flat figures come from. Changing the date is free while registration is still open.

### The standalone English test (AMIRNET)

From December 2026 this, not the psychometric, is how a candidate's university English level is established.

- Computer-administered test of English knowledge, using the same question types as the psychometric English section (sentence completion, restatement, reading comprehension)
- Scored **50-150**, and NITE states these scores are **equivalent to AMIR and AMIRAM scores and to the psychometric English-domain score**, so an existing AMIR/AMIRAM result is not wasted
- Fee **315 NIS**; date change 142 NIS
- Results in the personal area within about 10 days, downloadable free; a minimum of 35 days between sittings
- **From December 2026 you must register for AMIRNET separately, and pay for it separately.** Sitting the two-domain psychometric leaves the candidate with NO English score and therefore no university English placement until they book AMIRNET as its own test, on its own date, for its own fee. Say this explicitly to anyone planning a December 2026 or later sitting. A candidate who discovers the gap late cannot always fix it quickly: NITE requires at least 35 days between AMIRNET sittings.
- **NITE publishes no exemption (patur) threshold table.** Each institution decides what score exempts a student from English studies and at what level, and NITE explicitly warns that one institution's decision does not bind another. Never quote a universal patur cutoff.



## Student rights, retakes and accommodations (full table)



Beyond grades and scores, students have a layer of statutory rights administered by the Ministry of Education. These are commonly needed and easy to miss.

| Right | Details |
|-------|---------|
| Retake / grade improvement (moed bet, mo'ed meyuchad) | A student can re-sit a Bagrut exam to improve a grade; the higher grade counts. **Moed bet is a summer-session mechanism only, and only in English, Mathematics and mother tongue**; the winter session has no moed bet. In the תשפ"ז summer draft it falls on 5-8 July 2027. Special sessions exist for students who missed an exam for an approved reason (illness, bereavement, reserve duty). Improvement re-sits are available even after finishing school (as an external examinee). |
| Appeal an exam grade | The appeal window differs by track: **30 days** from publication of the official grade for school students, **21 days** for external examinees. The grade can go up, stay the same, or go **down**, so this is a real decision and not a free option. An extern deciding whether to appeal should request the scanned booklet immediately and no later than 10 days after publication, and then has up to 11 days from receiving it to file. |
| Appeal / exam-integrity review | Separate procedures exist if a student is suspected of an exam-integrity violation, with a right to a hearing. |
| Learning-disability accommodations | Students with a recognized diagnosis (ikui lemida) can receive accommodations: extra time, separate room, oral exam, reader/scribe, spelling leniency. Funding support for the diagnosis itself may be available. Apply through the school well before the exam. |
| Reserve-duty (miluim) accommodations | An exceptions committee sets accommodations (extended deadlines, special sessions, magen adjustments) for students, or those whose parents served, scaled by service length and proximity to the exam. These are granted on application, so a student who does not apply gets nothing. If a psychometric session is missed because of a call-up, ask NITE about a date change rather than forfeiting the fee. |
| New-immigrant / returning-resident adaptations | Beyond the Hebrew-level bonus, olim get adapted exam papers, extra time, and dictionary use for a defined period (see Step 2). |

**Deadlines for external examinees (nivchanim externiim):** opening an examinee file runs 1 September to 20 November for winter and 1 February to 20 March for summer. These are the EXTERNS' windows; a school student registers through the school on its own timetable, so do not quote these dates to one. Minimum age to sit is 16 years and 2 months. Approving an accommodation is not the same as arranging it: the regulations say a committee decision does not by itself secure implementation.

**The 2026/27 (תשפ"ז) exam calendar is published in draft.** The Ministry released draft winter and summer timetables on 12/07/2026, both stamped as subject to change. Winter תשפ"ז runs 27.12.2026 to 4.2.2027; summer תשפ"ז runs 25.4.2027 to 9.7.2027, with moed bet on 5-8.7.2027. Because they are drafts, read the specific subject date off the Ministry's current timetable rather than quoting it from here.

When advising a student, always check the Ministry of Education student portal and Kol Zchut for the current-year deadlines and forms, since these accommodations are time-bound.
