# Hebrew Document Glossary for Genealogical Research

## Birth Certificate (תעודת לידה)

| Hebrew | Transliteration | English |
|--------|----------------|---------|
| שם פרטי | Shem prati | First name |
| שם משפחה | Shem mishpacha | Family name |
| תאריך לידה | Ta'arikh leida | Date of birth |
| מקום לידה | Makom leida | Place of birth |
| שם האב | Shem ha'av | Father's name |
| שם האם | Shem ha'em | Mother's name |
| מספר זהות | Mispar zehut | ID number. The modern form is 9 digits with a check digit. Older documents often show a shorter written form and leading zeros are lost in transcription, so a length mismatch is not by itself a non-match. Confirm on name, parents and dates. |
| מין | Min | Gender (זכר = male, נקבה = female) |
| דת | Dat | Religion |
| לאום | Le'om | Nationality/Ethnicity. This field was removed from the printed teudat zehut in 2002, so its presence helps date a document. |
| ארץ לידה | Eretz leida | Country of birth |

## Hebrew Calendar Months

|--------|----------------|---------------------|
| תשרי | Tishrei | September-October |
| חשוון / מרחשוון | Cheshvan / Marcheshvan | October-November. מרחשוון is the formal name and is what appears in older documents. |
| כסלו | Kislev | November-December |
| טבת | Tevet | December-January |
| שבט | Shevat | January-February |
| אדר | Adar | February-March. In a LEAP year plain אדר does not occur; the year has two Adars instead. |
| אדר א' | Adar Alef | February-March (leap year only) |
| אדר ב' | Adar Bet | March (leap year only). A date in Adar of a leap year is ambiguous unless the document says Alef or Bet. |

## Common Abbreviations in Documents

| Abbreviation | Full Hebrew | English |
|-------------|-------------|---------|
| ת"ז | תעודת זהות | ID card |
| נ"ע | נשמתו/ה עדן | May their soul be in Eden (deceased). Do NOT read this as נ"צ, which in modern Israeli usage is נקודת ציון, a map grid reference, and is not a genealogical term. |
| ז"ל | זיכרונו/ה לברכה | Of blessed memory (deceased) |
| בע"מ | בערבון מוגבל | Ltd. (company) |
| ע"ר | עמותה רשומה | Registered association |
| ד"ר | דוקטור | Doctor |
| פרופ' | פרופסור | Professor |
| הרב | הרב | Rabbi |
| ב"ב | בני ביתו OR בני ברק | In pre-state and rabbinic documents this overwhelmingly reads בני ביתו, "his household". Only read it as the city Bnei Brak when the surrounding text is clearly a modern address. Getting this wrong relocates a family. |
| ת"א | תל אביב | Tel Aviv |
| י-ם | ירושלים | Jerusalem |
| תנצב"ה | תהא נשמתו/ה צרורה בצרור החיים | Closing formula on nearly every Jewish gravestone |
| נלב"ע | נפטר/ה לבית עולמו/ה | Died, passed away (introduces the death date on a matzevah) |
| ע"ה | עליו/ה השלום | May peace be upon them (deceased) |
| הי"ד | השם יקום דמו/ה | May God avenge their blood (murdered, incl. Holocaust victims) |
| ב"ר / בן ר' / בת ר' | בן/בת רבי | "son/daughter of R."  This is the FATHER'S NAME marker on a gravestone and in religious documents. It is the single highest-value field on a matzevah for extending a tree one generation. |
| הכהן / הלוי | הכהן / הלוי | Tribal status (Kohen / Levi). Genealogically load-bearing and often survives when a surname does not. |
| לפ"ק | לפרט קטן | "By the minor reckoning": the thousands digit (ה' = 5000) has been DROPPED. Near-universal on gravestones and ketubot. Without spotting it you read תשי"ח as 718 instead of 5718. |


## Reading a Hebrew Date (gematria)

Hebrew years and days are written as letters, not digits. To decode `ה'תשי"ח` you need the letter values,
not just the month table above.

| Letters | Value |
|---------|-------|
| א ב ג ד ה ו ז ח ט | 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9 |
| י כ ל מ נ ס ע פ צ | 10 20 30 40 50 60 70 80 90 |
| ק ר ש ת | 100 200 300 400 |

Final forms (ך ם ן ף ץ) carry the same value as their regular forms in dates. The gershayim (`"`) before the
last letter marks the string as a number rather than a word; a single geresh (`'`) after a letter marks the
thousands digit.

**Worked example:** `ה'תשי"ח` = ה' (5000) + ת (400) + ש (300) + י (10) + ח (8) = 5718.

**The two traps that break naive decoding:**

1. **ט"ו = 15 and ט"ז = 16**, not י"ה and י"ו. The obvious combinations are avoided because they spell divine
   names. A reader expecting י"ה and not finding it concludes the day is illegible.
2. **לפ"ק (לפרט קטן)** means the thousands digit was dropped. `תשי"ח לפ"ק` is 5718, not 718. This is the norm
   on gravestones and ketubot, so assume the current millennium (5000) unless the text says otherwise.

**Converting to Gregorian.** State the constant explicitly rather than saying "approximately":

- Tishrei through 31 December: subtract **3761**
- 1 January through Elul: subtract **3760**

So 5718 spans September 1957 (Tishrei 5718) through September 1958. A Hebrew date with no month cannot be
pinned to a single Gregorian year at all, so give the user the two-year span rather than picking one. Verify
any converted date with a Hebrew-Gregorian converter before you record it.
